01This is Workflow0:50
"So what is it? One line: Workflow is BillOne's approval engine. Any time something needs a human to say yes, an invoice to pay, an expense to reimburse, Workflow decides who approves, in what order, and records it. It's built and run by our WF group. But it didn't start as an engine. It started much smaller."
Gesture to the Workflow character. Let "started much smaller" set up the story.
02How it began · Invoice + 承認くん1:00
"First, there was Invoice. BillOne receives an invoice and files it, but before anyone pays, someone has to approve it. So an approval feature was built, wrapping BillOne's existing sign feature, right inside the invoice service. That approval helper is who we'll call 承認くん. Invoice says 'someone approve this!' and he says 'はい！', on it."
Invoice is already asking. On your cue ("let's call him 承認くん… はい！"), click the slide to pop 承認くん in. He's the character the whole talk hangs on, point at him.
03He grew up0:55
"And 承認くん grew up. That little feature became a real approval system, serial routes that step through an order, approver groups so you route to a team or a role, proxy approval, and send-back to reject and return a step. All of it living inside the invoice service, sharing its database. Quietly, he became critical to how invoices get paid."
Slightly proud tone, he's a capable little guy now.
04The catch · stuck inside1:00
"But here's the catch. 承認くん only ever knew invoices. He was invoice-shaped, wired straight into the invoice database, speaking only invoice's language, its IDs, its tables. No automatic approval; every step waited on a human. And because he'd been built in a hurry, feature by feature as needs came up, he carried heavy technical debt. On top of that, he was unshareable, no other service could reuse a single line of him. Great for invoices. Invisible to everything else."
Slow down. Pause after "invisible to everything else." This is the low point.
05Then came Model41:00
"Then, under Sansan's Model4 strategy, two new services arrive, Expense and Issuing, and both need approval. Not a nice-to-have, a must-have. 'Hey, I'd love an approval flow too!' But 承認くん was locked inside Invoice, debt and all. So we had a choice: copy-paste approval into each, or build it right. We built it right, a standalone Workflow microservice any service can call. Expense became its first customer. And now we had two approval worlds."
Three clicks: (1) the Model4 · 2024 chip, (2) Expense + Issuing rise in ("We'd love approval too!"), (3) Workflow answers. Pace it to your narration.
06Table stakes · Kano0:45
"Here's why that mattered. In Kano-model terms, approval is a must-be quality. Nobody thanks Expense or Issuing for having an approval flow, it's invisible when it's there. But ship them without it? Dead on arrival. So for these services, workflow wasn't optional, it was table stakes."
Let the bright must-be curve draw, then land "dead on arrival." Point to Workflow sitting in the dissatisfier zone.
07Why it matters · two of him0:50
"And two worlds is the problem. Effectively two 承認くん, one trapped in Invoice, one in the new service. Two implementations of 'approval' means double the maintenance, and worse, the rules drift apart, the same company could get different behavior in different services. And unifying approval for just Expense and Issuing wouldn't fix it, you'd still lack consistency across the whole product. In a finance product, that's exactly what you can't have, which is why WF had to be one service, for every module."
This is the tension, sound a little concerned here.
08Where WF fits · one for everyone1:00
"So where should this live? Not as a feature inside one service, as a layer under many. One unified Workflow that Invoice, Expense, and whatever comes next all plug into. That's our group's mission: a unified workflow foundation. Technically, services reach it two ways, a synchronous internal API through a client library, and async domain events over PubSub, like WorkflowRequestStatusChanged, so a service knows the instant its request is approved. And it's not just plumbing, the goal is a WF that's genuinely easy to use for admins, approvers, and every user, and that automates away the tedious apply-and-approve busywork."
The turn toward the solution, lift the energy. The two integration paths are your technical credibility.
09Where it's going · 承認くん can rest0:50
"Which means 承認くん can finally rest. The plan: Invoice migrates its approval onto the shared service, the same one Expense already uses. It's already partway there, with a module that delegates to the Workflow client library. The old embedded flow retires; everyone plugs into one core."
Warm payoff beat, he earned his rest.
10What it unlocks · interactive1:30
"And its own engine unlocks things the embedded flow never could. Three quick ones.

[Parallel] Within a segment you get multiple branches, approvers working at once, and each branch is itself a sequence of steps. All branches finish, then a serial step, because a route can't end on a parallel.

[Automatic] Rule-based steps that approve themselves when the rule matches the evidence, but at least one human step is always required. This one's still in progress on our side.

[Relay] And relay, careful here: relay itself isn't new, the old WF had it. What's new is combining relay with routes. Each approver nominates the next, until the final one completes.

[Org chart] You set the route template once, then the actual approvers are resolved live from the org chart, so nobody hand-edits routes when people change roles or move departments. Fun aside, that same org chart is even used in Expense's daily-allowance flows."
Click each tab as you name it; let the animation finish, then explain. Say "in progress" out loud for Automatic.
11Plot twist0:55
"Now, plot twist. 承認くん isn't going anywhere just yet. For some tenants, the old flow is genuinely enough, they don't need parallel or automatic approval, and simpler suits their use case. Realistically, full migration is two to three years out. But make no mistake, our stance as the Workflow group is unification. One Workflow, eventually for everyone."
On "…but he isn't going anywhere," click to pop 承認くん back in. Land it lightly for a laugh, then get sincere on "unification."

"So that's the story. Workflow started as a feature inside Invoice, and grew into BillOne's shared approval backbone, the layer that turns received into approved, for whatever comes next. Thank you. And if you build something that needs approval, you know who to call: the WF group."
